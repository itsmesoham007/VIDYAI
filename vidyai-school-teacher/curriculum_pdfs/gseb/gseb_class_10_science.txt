## Chapter 1: Chemical Reactions and Equations
- **Syllabus Identifier**: GSEB-ENG-STD10-SCI-CH01
- **Board**: GSEB (English Medium)
- **Standard**: 10
- **Stream**: Secondary
- **Subject**: Science & Technology
- **Core Concepts & Learning Outcomes**: Balancing chemical equations, combination, decomposition, displacement, double displacement, redox, rancidity

### Comprehensive Study Notes: Chemical Reactions and Equations

A chemical reaction involves breaking existing bonds between reactant atoms and forming new chemical bonds to produce distinct products with new properties.

#### Types of Chemical Reactions:
1. **Combination Reaction**: Two or more reactants combine to form a single product. Example: Slaking of lime: CaO(s) + H2O(l) -> Ca(OH)2(aq) + Heat.
2. **Decomposition Reaction**: A single reactant breaks down into multiple simpler products upon application of heat, light, or electricity. Example: 2FeSO4(s) --(Heat)--> Fe2O3(s) + SO2(g) + SO3(g).
3. **Displacement Reaction**: A more reactive metal displaces a less reactive metal from its aqueous salt solution. Example: Fe(s) + CuSO4(aq) [blue] -> FeSO4(aq) [pale green] + Cu(s) [red-brown].
4. **Double Displacement (Precipitation) Reaction**: Mutual exchange of ions between reactants. Example: Na2SO4(aq) + BaCl2(aq) -> BaSO4(s) [white precipitate] + 2NaCl(aq).
5. **Redox (Oxidation-Reduction) Reaction**: Simultaneous oxidation (gain of oxygen / loss of electrons) and reduction (loss of oxygen / gain of electrons). Example: CuO + H2 --(Heat)--> Cu + H2O (CuO is reduced to Cu; H2 is oxidized to H2O).


### GSEB Board Examination Focus & High-Yield Q&A:

**GSEB 2-Mark Question**: Differentiate between a Combination Reaction and a Decomposition Reaction with balanced equations.
- **Model Answer**: In combination, two reactants combine into one: CaO + H2O -> Ca(OH)2 + Heat. In decomposition, one reactant breaks down into simpler products: 2FeSO4 --(Heat)--> Fe2O3 + SO2 + SO3.

## Chapter 4: Carbon and its Compounds
- **Syllabus Identifier**: GSEB-ENG-STD10-SCI-CH04
- **Board**: GSEB (English Medium)
- **Standard**: 10
- **Stream**: Secondary
- **Subject**: Science & Technology
- **Core Concepts & Learning Outcomes**: Covalent bonding, tetravalency, catenation, homologous series, functional groups, ethanol and ethanoic acid

### Comprehensive Study Notes: Electricity and Circuit Laws

#### 1. Ohm's Law:
At constant temperature, the electric current (I) flowing through a metallic conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference (V) across its ends:
$$V = I \cdot R$$
where R is the resistance of the conductor in ohms (\Omega). Resistance depends on material resistivity (\rho), length (L), and cross-sectional area (A): $R = \rho \frac{L}{A}$.

#### 2. Resistors in Series and Parallel:
- **Series Combination**: Current remains constant throughout; equivalent resistance $R_s = R_1 + R_2 + R_3$.
- **Parallel Combination**: Potential difference remains constant; equivalent resistance $\frac{1}{R_p} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} + \frac{1}{R_3}$.

#### 3. Joule's Law of Heating:
Heat produced in a resistor is directly proportional to square of current, resistance, and time: $H = I^2 R t$ joules.
