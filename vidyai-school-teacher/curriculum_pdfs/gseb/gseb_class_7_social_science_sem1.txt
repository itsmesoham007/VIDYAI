## Chapter 3: The Mughal Empire: Golden Age of Architecture
- **Syllabus Identifier**: GSEB-ENG-STD07-SOC-CH03
- **Board**: GSEB (English Medium)
- **Standard**: 7
- **Stream**: Middle
- **Subject**: Social Science (Semester 1 - Medieval India & Mughal Era)
- **Core Concepts & Learning Outcomes**: Emperors Babur, Humayun, Akbar (Din-i-Ilahi), Jahangir, Shah Jahan (Prince of Builders, Taj Mahal, Red Fort, Peacock Throne), Aurangzeb

### Comprehensive Study Notes: The Mughal Empire (1526–1707 CE)

The Mughal Empire established a unified imperial administration over India for over two centuries. Major rulers and their contributions include:

1. **Babur (1526–1530 CE)**: Founder of the Mughal Empire. Defeated Sultan Ibrahim Lodi in the First Battle of Panipat (1526) using field artillery. Authored his famous autobiography 'Tuzuk-i-Baburi' in Chagatai Turkic.
2. **Humayun (1530–1556 CE)**: Succeeded Babur. Temporarily ousted by Afghan ruler **Sher Shah Suri** (who built the Grand Trunk Road and introduced the silver 'Rupiya'). Humayun recaptured Delhi in 1555.
3. **Akbar the Great (1556–1605 CE)**: Crowned at age 13 after winning the Second Battle of Panipat (1556). Implemented the **Mansabdari** military system, revenue measurement system under finance minister Raja Todar Mal, policy of universal religious tolerance (**Sulh-i-Kul**), and founded the synthetic spiritual movement **Din-i-Ilahi**. Built Agra Fort, Lahore Fort, and the capital city of Fatehpur Sikri with the colossal Buland Darwaza.
4. **Jahangir (1605–1627 CE)**: Known for his strict administration of justice (installed the Golden Chain of Justice at Agra Fort). Patronized exquisite Mughal miniature painting.
5. **Emperor Shah Jahan (1628–1658 CE)**: Fifth Mughal Emperor, celebrated universally as the **'Prince of Builders'**. His reign represents the **Golden Age of Mughal Architecture**. Built the world-famous **Taj Mahal** in Agra in memory of his wife Mumtaz Mahal, the **Red Fort** (Lal Qila) in Delhi, the **Jama Masjid** in Delhi, and the jewel-laden **Peacock Throne** (Takht-i-Taus). Transitioned Mughal building style to pure white Makrana marble and pietra dura gemstone inlays.
6. **Aurangzeb (1658–1707 CE)**: Expanded the empire across the Deccan plateau. Imprisoned his father Shah Jahan in Agra Fort. His orthodox policies and prolonged Deccan campaigns exhausted imperial resources, leading to the rapid fragmentation of the empire after his death in 1707 CE.


### GSEB Board Examination Focus & High-Yield Q&A:

**GSEB 2-Mark Question**: Name the major architectural monuments built by Mughal Emperor Shah Jahan.
- **Model Answer**: Shah Jahan built the Taj Mahal in Agra (dedicated to Mumtaz Mahal), the Red Fort (Lal Qila) in Delhi, the Jama Masjid in Delhi, and the Peacock Throne.

**GSEB 2-Mark Question**: Who founded the Mughal Empire in India, and when?
- **Model Answer**: Babur founded the Mughal Empire in 1526 CE after defeating Sultan Ibrahim Lodi in the First Battle of Panipat.
